## The sheet, and where its authority ends

The restoration-knowledge sheet is built from two sources of
unequal weight, and says so on its face. **Part A — IICRC
published facts**, read from the certifying body's own public
pages and webstore: exam fee $80, pass mark 75%, the CEC schedule,
Certified Firm $199/yr, and the edition pins — ANSI/IICRC S500
**Fifth Edition, 2021**; ANSI/IICRC S520 **Fourth Edition, 2024**
— plus IICRC's own framing that its standards are voluntary
consensus documents, *not laws and not licences*. **Part B — the
desk's own records**: the September 26 citation census figures,
self-attested and labeled as such, usable only to check pages
against the desk's own published numbers. The sheet contains **no
IICRC standards text and never will** — the standards are sold,
subscription-held documents; published facts and edition pins
only. And it names its largest gap plainly: **state radon
statutes are not covered** — the radon cluster's core claims sit
outside this sheet's jurisdiction entirely.

## The widening, stated

Step 6's lesson is about the *shape* of jurisdiction beyond
vendors. A vendor sheet's authority is uniform — every price on
the page answers to it. This sheet's authority is **patchy by
design**: strong on IICRC's published facts, self-referential on
the desk's own numbers (it can catch the desk contradicting
itself, never the desk being wrong about the world), and absent
on the state statutes the radon cluster is actually about. The
loop's yield followed the patches exactly — silence where the
sheet was absent, two real catches where it was self-referential.
The next jurisdiction of this kind would be built the same way
the need appears: a state-statute sheet is a research project of
its own (fifty jurisdictions, primary sources), and tonight's
35-item catalogue is its scope document, waiting.
