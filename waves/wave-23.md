## The verdict, priced

Wave 21's sheet cost its wave's entire first phase (~25 primary
reads) to settle a 35-item catalogue across 23 uniform pages.
This cluster's shape is the opposite: **62% of its claims sit
permanently outside any jurisdiction this program can build** —
the procedural core answers to standards text that is not
public, and a third of the claims are an operator's experience,
which no agency settles. Full restoration-core jurisdiction is
not a budget problem; it is structurally unavailable.

What IS available is narrow and cheap: an EPA/CDC/FEMA
public-guidance sheet (~5 primary reads — the agencies publish
consolidated guidance, not fifty statutes) covering **51 claims
on about ten pages**, concentrated in the two crawl-space mold
guides. That reduced sheet was worth building, and was built.
The verdict the log records: *step 9's jurisdiction is a shelf,
not a territory — the public-guidance layer only, with the
standards-text core named as the permanent boundary.*

## The loop under the reduced sheet

Four carriers audited (the two mold guides, the photo page, the
Puyallup guide): **2 findings, both on the crawl-space mold
guide, both shipped.** Its remediation protocol prescribed a
registered antimicrobial application as a standard step; EPA's
guidance is detergent-and-water scrubbing and says biocides are
not a routine part of cleanup, because dead mold still causes
allergic reactions. Its when-to-hire-a-professional list advised
testing when "the mold type is unknown"; EPA and CDC both say
sampling is unnecessary when visible mold is present and
removal is warranted regardless of type — the trigger now reads
as suspected hidden mold. Around them, 72 claims stayed
NEEDS-VERIFY under the sheet's own bounded absences (species
toxicology rankings, exact growth thresholds, the page's 25–30%
coverage trigger — none of which the agencies' public guidance
settles), exactly the discipline the catalogue priced in.
